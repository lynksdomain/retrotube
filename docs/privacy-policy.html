RetroTube Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

RetroTube is a local video player. It does not have accounts, does not run ads, and does not include any analytics, tracking, or crash-reporting SDKs. This page explains, plainly, what the app does and doesn't do with your data.

What RetroTube accesses

  • Video files you choose. RetroTube only reads folders you explicitly grant it access to via Android's Storage Access Framework. It never scans your device's storage on its own.
  • Network shares you connect. If you add an SMB/NAS share, RetroTube connects directly to the host and share you enter (address, share name, username/password). That connection is used only to browse and play files from that share.

What RetroTube stores, and where

  • Everything stays on your device. RetroTube has no backend server. Library folders, playback progress, custom titles/tags/posters, collections, TV Mode channel setup, and shader preferences are all stored locally in the app's own private storage.
  • Network share credentials (host, share name, username, password) are stored on-device using Android's EncryptedSharedPreferences (AES-256-GCM, key held in the Android Keystore) — never in plain text, and never sent anywhere except directly to the share you configured.

What RetroTube never does

  • No account creation or sign-in.
  • No analytics, telemetry, or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No ads or ad SDKs.
  • No data is ever sent to RetroTube's developer or any third party. There is no server to send it to.

Permissions

RetroTube requests INTERNET only to reach an SMB share you explicitly connect to on your own local network — never anything beyond that. It does not request broad storage permissions; local video access goes through per-folder Storage Access Framework grants that you approve individually.

Open source

RetroTube is open source (GPL-3.0). You're welcome to read the actual source code — including everything described above — at github.com/lynksdomain/retrotube.
